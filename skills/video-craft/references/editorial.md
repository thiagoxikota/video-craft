# Editorial decisions

## One brief, one promise

Write a sentence describing what viewers should understand or feel at the end. Identify the strongest real moment before choosing a template. A technology recap can move from an intriguing action, to people collaborating, to a visible result and a grounded invitation. A tutorial needs readable steps; a product demo needs cause and effect. Do not force every genre into a trailer.

Build a shot map with these columns: source file, in/out, subject/action, narrative role, original audio, graphic need, rights/source. Alternate scale (wide/detail/face), action and direction. Cut because meaning changes or an action resolves; the beat is a useful anchor, not an obligation.

For a short recap, save the strongest visual or reaction for a payoff. Do not hide useful footage behind text in every shot. Let the first frame establish action, not an empty title card.

## Graphics with a job

A place can become an original vector map or a labeled route. A number can become a counter with its unit and source. A workflow can become a small connected diagram. A partner can be represented by its official logo if its use is permitted. Choose one dominant idea per moment. Avoid repeating an identical device until it feels procedural.

For a map, verify coordinates and projection. Frame the relevant geography deliberately; do not silently place a marker using a previous project's coordinates. A diagram may use connecting lines; a decorative line adds no explanation by itself.

Use size, weight and space for text hierarchy. Make supporting text legible over the brightest plausible video frame. A scrim can help, but inspect the actual composited frame. Keep captions away from app controls and faces, then preview in the intended destination. Safe areas depend on the channel and change over time; verify before delivery.

## Motion and timing

Motion should establish hierarchy, continuity or state. Use a small vocabulary of transitions. Match direction and acceleration across cuts; vary composition rather than adding a different effect to each shot. For an overlap transition, keep the outgoing clip alive under the incoming layer to avoid an unintended black frame.

At frame rate F, time t maps to frame round(t × F). Define beat/cut positions from the actual music, not an inherited BPM constant. If the music changes, revisit the edit grid. Never apply a hardcoded audio offset from another machine or version; test synchronization with a recognizable transient.

Avoid rapid full-screen flashes and strobing. Human review is still necessary: no check in this repository certifies flash safety or WCAG conformance. Prefer calm readable captions, enough reading time and meaningful alternatives for spoken or visually essential information.

## Review in context

Watch once for narrative, once for framing/text, once with eyes closed for sound. Then inspect the opening, ending and every transition. Check repeated shots, accidental black frames, compression artifacts and dead time. Do not make the user discover a broken export before you open it.
